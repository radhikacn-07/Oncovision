Breast Awareness

Self-Examination Guidance

Take a few minutes regularly to become familiar with what is normal for you.

Expert guidance

Know what is normal for you

Breast awareness means becoming familiar with how your breasts or chest usually look and feel, so a new change is easier to notice without becoming anxious about normal differences.

Look

Use a mirror with arms relaxed and then raised. Notice a new change in size, shape, symmetry, skin texture or nipple appearance.

Feel gently

Use flat finger pads with light and firmer pressure. Cover the breast or chest, collarbone area and underarm without pressing hard enough to hurt.

Check a change

Most changes are not cancer, but a new lump, persistent focal pain, dimpling, nipple inversion, unusual discharge or swelling should be assessed.

Awareness—not diagnosis. There is no single perfect checking technique. Self-checking does not replace mammogram screening or professional assessment; do not wait for the next screening appointment if you notice a new change.
